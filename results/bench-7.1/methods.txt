source-hashes.csv records the final sources, tests and build definitions.

Every session used the five 7.0 jobs, seed 20261119, log level 1, no
tracking, 25 RKM repeats and 300 RS swaps. Four rounds alternated the
after and before binaries, and all 64 run/seed/SSE/CI identities matched.
The laptop was on AC power. The sums of per-run minima are:
  compiler  session    after ms    before ms    after/before
  MinGW       1        6206.381     6263.405       0.990896
  MinGW       2        6173.697     6234.750       0.990208
  MinGW       3        6166.854     6179.717       0.997919
  MSVC        1        7270.348     7455.272       0.975196
These small gains and individual dataset ratios include timing noise.
comparison.csv groups the same minima by dataset and algorithm; the
session CSVs retain every sample identity and both minima.

The first scratch-only MSVC build was slower: 8673.512 ms versus 7407.237
ms in a paired session. Its nearest-centroid helper remained out of line.
Forcing that existing hot helper inline on MSVC restored the gain above;
the MinGW implementation remains standard inline. A clean MSVC rebuild
showed the same initial regression. This is measured compiler sensitivity,
not a claim about its exact microarchitectural cause.

The final MinGW/MSVC benchmark also matched all 64 identities. Its
minima total 6133.152 ms MinGW and 7245.222 ms MSVC, retained separately
in cross-platform.csv; those are not a before/after comparison. All 21
input hashes remained unchanged.
